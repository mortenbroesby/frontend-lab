# Ideas to explore

Build something because it sparks your curiosity. Start with one interaction, use invented data where helpful and let the idea grow only as far as you want. These suggestions are invitations, not assignments.

## A small browser game

Try a reaction game, a chapter quiz, a puzzle or a playful scoring mechanic. Start with one rule and a way to restart. A single enjoyable interaction is enough for a first result.

Add keyboard controls, a second player or a new rule when the core feels good. Explore what changes when feedback comes through animation, sound or timing.

Read: [MDN’s game-development guides](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Games).

## Generative art

Turn a few shapes and some randomness into a postcard, a pattern or a moving composition. Explore how colour, repetition and constraints change the result.

Start with a small collection of SVG or Canvas shapes and one control. Try repeatable random seeds, pointer interaction or exporting an image. Compare the same idea using different drawing techniques.

Read: [Canvas tutorial](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial) and [D3’s getting-started guide](https://d3js.org/getting-started).

## Interactive stories and visualisations

Make a small dataset or story explorable. It could be a map of interests, a branching narrative, an illustrated explanation or a collection that changes as you filter it.

Begin with a single relationship or choice. Experiment with selection, layout and transitions, then consider how the story works without animation or visual cues.

Read: [D3](https://d3js.org/getting-started) and [View Transitions](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API).

## Shared and unusual experiences

Combine drawing, sound or communication in something you would not normally build at work. Try a collaborative sketch, a tiny musical instrument or a strange but useful utility for your squad.

Start locally with one observable behaviour. Then connect two browsers or combine contributions from a small group. Investigate the hardest constraint before adding accounts, persistence or more infrastructure.

Read: [Web Audio API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API) and [WebRTC](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebRTC_API).

Bring your own React idea too. You can ignore every suggestion above.
