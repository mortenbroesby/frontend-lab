<Inspiration>

Spend a few minutes playing with something before choosing what to build:

- **Make a game:** [MDN’s Canvas Breakout tutorial](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Games/Tutorials/2D_breakout_game_pure_JavaScript) includes playable examples of a paddle-and-ball game, with steps from moving a ball to keeping score.
- **Make music:** [Chrome Music Lab](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Experiments) turns sound and rhythm into things you can play with. Try Song Maker and think about a tiny instrument of your own.
- **Make something beautiful:** [WebGL Fluid Simulation](https://paveldogreat.github.io/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation/) lets you play with flowing colour. It is an ambitious visual reference; a simple Canvas brush or particle trail is a smaller place to begin.
- **Make an idea understandable:** [Red Blob Games’ interactive pathfinding explanation](https://www.redblobgames.com/pathfinding/a-star/introduction.html) lets you move points and explore how routes are found. Imagine explaining something you know through interaction.

Pick one interaction you like and give it your own twist. These are inspiration, not a checklist of finished projects to reproduce. Get that first interaction working before adding levels, multiplayer or more sophisticated visuals.

</Inspiration>

<IdeaGrid>

<Idea title="A game you can play in a minute" summary="Build a paddle game, a tiny maze or a one-button challenge. Make one interaction feel good.">

Make a paddle game, a one-button obstacle course or a tiny maze. What makes someone want to try again? Explore movement, timing, collision and the feeling of a good control.

Start with a ball, a paddle and a restart button, or a character that can reach one goal. Canvas works well for drawing a moving scene; React can handle the score and controls around it. Stretch with a second player on the same keyboard, changing obstacles or touch controls.

Build from [MDN’s Canvas Breakout steps](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Games/Tutorials/2D_breakout_game_pure_JavaScript). The examples use plain JavaScript; use their drawing and game-loop ideas inside your React demo rather than expecting a React starter.

</Idea>

<Idea title="An ecosystem in a tab" summary="Give creatures simple rules, add food and watch surprising behaviour emerge.">

Give tiny creatures a few simple rules and watch a world emerge. Plant food, change the weather or introduce something they should avoid. What do they seek? What happens when food becomes scarce?

Start with one creature following food. Explore flocking, predators or changing seasons when that becomes interesting. Surprising behaviour can come from very little code.

Read: [MDN’s animation-loop examples](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Basic_animations).

</Idea>

<Idea title="An impossible instrument" summary="Turn shapes, colours or movement into an instrument you can play.">

Draw a shape and hear it. Let position control pitch, colour change the sound, or movement create a rhythm. Could a drawing become a song someone else can play?

Start with one sound triggered by a deliberate interaction. Explore loops, layered instruments or a visual sequencer. Give people a mute control and keep the first sound gentle.

Read: [Build a simple synth with Web Audio](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Simple_synth).

</Idea>

<Idea title="A machine nobody needs" summary="Build a satisfying chain reaction with switches, motion and an unexpected ending.">

Make a beautifully satisfying contraption: switches, bouncing balls and ridiculous chain reactions. A button might launch an elaborate machine whose only job is to switch itself off.

Start with one cause and one effect. Add timing, sound or an unexpected ending. How much personality can you give an ordinary button?

Read: [Using the Web Animations API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Animations_API/Using_the_Web_Animations_API).

</Idea>

<Idea title="A living city" summary="Draw a road and watch a miniature city respond to your decisions.">

Sketch a road and watch a miniature city wake up. People travel, traffic collects and your decisions change the flow.

Start with dots moving along one road. Try intersections, public transport or a strange rule: what if everyone always took the scenic route? Invent the data; realism is optional.

Read: [SVG paths](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Tutorials/SVG_from_scratch/Paths).

</Idea>

<Idea title="A world built by its visitors" summary="Let visitors contribute to a shared landscape, creature or constellation.">

Each visitor adds a tiny piece to a shared landscape, creature or constellation. Can a group make something none of them would have designed alone?

Begin by placing objects locally. Then connect browsers or combine contributions. Explore what happens when two people change the same thing; accounts and permanent storage can wait.

Read: [WebRTC data channels](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebRTC_API/Using_data_channels).

</Idea>

<Idea title="A picture made from rules" summary="Generate art from simple rules, then change the rules and see what happens.">

Grow imaginary coral, draw a fictional alphabet or generate maps of places that do not exist. Each variation should feel related, but different.

Start with one rule and a slider. Explore repeatable seeds, branching growth or artwork you can export. What happens when you break just one of your rules?

Read: [Canvas drawing shapes](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Canvas_API/Tutorial/Drawing_shapes).

</Idea>

<Idea title="A browser escape room" summary="Create an interactive puzzle with clues that players can inspect and connect.">

Hide clues in an interactive environment. Let players manipulate, inspect and connect things until a satisfying solution appears.

Start with one puzzle. Try linked rooms, cooperative clues or multiple solutions. Can someone solve it with a keyboard, without relying on colour or sound alone?

Read: [MDN’s game-development techniques](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Games/Techniques).

</Idea>

<Idea title="A playable explanation" summary="Explain an idea through interaction: change a variable and make its effect visible.">

Make someone feel how traffic jams form, how sound waves combine or how an algorithm makes a decision. Let them change a variable and discover something unexpected.

Start with one relationship and one control. Add a challenge or compare two scenarios. What does your model leave out, and does that change the story?

Read: [D3 scales for mapping data to visuals](https://d3js.org/d3-scale).

</Idea>

</IdeaGrid>

Choose the smallest version you would enjoy trying yourself.
