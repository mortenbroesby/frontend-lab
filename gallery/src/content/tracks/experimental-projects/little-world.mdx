## Invent a small world

A creature seeking shade, a crowd avoiding obstacles, a machine reacting to your cursor: choose a behaviour you're curious about.

Play with <DemoLink id="ecosystem-simulation">Little world</DemoLink>. Change food or sensing, then imagine a rule it doesn't have.

### Separate the rule from the picture

A simulation has **state**, a **rule for change** and a **display**. Describe your rule in plain language. What must an entity know to follow it? What happens at an edge or when it reaches its goal?

In `demos/ecosystem-simulation/`, explore `world.ts` for rules, `index.tsx` for controls and rendering, and `world.test.ts` for small behavioural examples. Adapt the demo or [create your own experiment](#/create).

Use the [SVG tutorial](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Tutorial) for drawing and [requestAnimationFrame](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/requestAnimationFrame) for updates.

### Give time a meaning

Does your world advance in turns or continuously? Fixed movement per frame ties speed to refresh rate; elapsed time separates them. Decide what should happen after a background tab resumes. Preserve loop cleanup and a way to pause or restart.

Check a few predictable states: near an edge, at a target or between competing targets. Then change one condition. Does more speed cause overshooting? Do more entities cooperate or compete?

Follow the behaviour that interests you. A single surprising rule is enough.
